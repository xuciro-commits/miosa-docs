There are two ways to read the Xici, one poor and one excellent. The poor reading treats it as a fortune manual: sixty-four configurations map to luck, and each line is a stroke of fate. The excellent reading treats it as a **formal treatise**. It describes how a system splits polarity out of an undifferentiated initial state, how a pair of conjugate operators drives its evolution, how irreducible evolution produces genuinely novel qualities, and how a cognitive agent must build a model isomorphic to the world in order to intervene before a bifurcation happens.

These pages are the second reading, written down. They do not ask anyone to believe anything; they lay out propositions, operators and inference chains, and leave the reader to judge whether the structure holds and whether it is useful.

## What this set covers

- [The Canonical Treatise in Twenty Chapters](/docs/xici/canon): the canonical text, stripped of divination and transliteration, keeping only the propositions.
- [The Architectonic Exegesis](/docs/xici/commentary/01-dao-vessel): each chapter glossed through computational ontology, non-equilibrium thermodynamics, world models and active inference.
- [The Lexicon](/docs/xici/lexicon): twenty core terms, their canonical English rendering, the rejected alternatives, and the reasoning behind each choice.
- [Symbols and the Five Master Equations](/docs/xici/equations): the whole work collapsed into an operator algebra and a closed loop of five coupled equations.

## A suggested reading path

1. Start with the canon. Twenty chapters are short, but they are the skeleton of an axiom system; skip them and the commentary loses its anchor.
2. Then read the books of the exegesis that match your field: people who build models tend to be struck by Books III and IV, while people working on control and alignment stop at Book V.
3. Keep the lexicon at hand as a reference. Whenever a term feels mistranslated, look up the reasoning first.
4. Finish with the formal apparatus, to confirm this is not a pile of handsome metaphors but something that can be written as equations.

## Conventions

| Mark | Meaning | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| Lexicon | A strict modern definition of an old term, one word to one concept | Every chapter of the exegesis |
| Exegesis | The chapter placed inside a modern theoretical frame, with its consequences drawn out | Every chapter of the exegesis |
| Canonical rendering | The English term this set uses throughout; never mixed with alternatives | Every page |
| Rejected rendering | An older translation dropped because it imports ambiguity; listed for reference | The lexicon |

> **One self-imposed constraint**
>
> This set introduces no unverifiable assertion. Wherever it says "necessarily", the claim must land on an operator, a gradient or an inequality. Where no such landing exists, the claim is weakened to "holds within this framework".
